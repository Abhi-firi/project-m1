How I Would Evaluate and Enter the Indian Healthcare Market for Bolna AI

Case study 2 of 6 in my "Proof of Thinking" series — demonstrating how I reason through growth problems using real frameworks, disclosed assumptions and no fabricated data.

Important Disclaimer

This is an independent strategic recommendation based on publicly available information about Bolna AI and general market-sizing and go-to-market principles. It is not affiliated with Bolna and does not use its internal sales, pricing or customer data.

All market-sizing figures, customer counts, annual spend assumptions, revenue scenarios and ROI examples in this case study are hypothetical planning assumptions. They are not Bolna's actual targets, internal data or forecasts.

01Executive Summary

India's healthcare sector handles a large volume of patient interactions, from appointment inquiries and reminders to follow-ups and routine information requests. Some of these workflows are repetitive, time-sensitive and dependent on phone calls. For healthcare providers operating across multiple locations, managing these interactions consistently can become operationally demanding.

Bolna offers a platform for building, deploying and monitoring AI voice agents. Its potential value in healthcare is not simply that an AI agent can make or answer calls — the value is whether it can help a healthcare provider manage an eligible workflow more efficiently while maintaining patient trust, appropriate escalation and reliable operations.

My recommendation: test a focused market-entry strategy targeting multi-location private diagnostic chains and outpatient-focused healthcare groups in India, starting with appointment reminders, confirmations and routine scheduling inquiries — not every hospital, clinic and healthcare institution.

The strategy has five parts:

  1. Estimate the addressable market using a transparent bottom-up model.
  2. Prioritize a narrow ICP and select an initial geographic beachhead.
  3. Validate demand through customer discovery and paid pilots.
  4. Prove commercial value through operational and financial metrics.
  5. Scale only after the product, economics and sales motion demonstrate repeatability.

The working scenario in this case aims to acquire 150 healthcare customers over three years, subject to validation of market size, annual spend, sales capacity and implementation requirements. This is a strategic planning scenario — not a forecast, an announced Bolna target or a claim about its actual healthcare business.

The central question: can Bolna establish a repeatable healthcare acquisition motion that delivers measurable customer value and attractive recurring revenue?

02Understanding the Company and the Market-Entry Problem

Bolna's public positioning describes a platform for building, deploying and monitoring voice AI agents, with use cases spanning e-commerce, recruitment, logistics and healthcare, and multilingual voice support. Its published pricing includes usage-based options and customized enterprise plans — which matters, since healthcare customers may differ significantly in call volume, integration requirements, support needs and willingness to commit to usage.

I would not assume Bolna's existing healthcare go-to-market is weak. Instead I would investigate whether healthcare can support a more focused vertical sales motion, testing four hypotheses:

  • Some multi-location providers handle enough routine calls to justify automation.
  • Appointment-related workflows may offer measurable outcomes — fewer missed calls, less manual effort.
  • Healthcare buyers may need more trust, integration support and operational safeguards than simpler use cases.
  • A narrowly defined segment may be easier to sell to than the healthcare market as a whole.

Each hypothesis requires customer discovery and real operating data to confirm.

Decision principle: Market size is not sufficient evidence to enter a market. The segment must be reachable, willing to pay, operationally feasible and commercially attractive.

04Define the Ideal Customer Profile (ICP)

The initial target would be a private diagnostic chain or outpatient-focused provider with multiple operating locations or a centralized patient-contact function, meaningful inbound appointment inquiries or outbound reminder activity, existing scheduling/customer-management processes, a clearly identified operations or contact-centre owner, a measurable problem handling calls, the technical ability and organizational willingness to test an AI voice agent, and a defined escalation process to human staff.

I would deprioritize accounts where call volume is too low, there's no clear workflow owner, the buyer can't identify a measurable problem, the use case requires unsafe autonomous clinical decision-making, integration requirements exceed available capability, or the organization is unwilling to establish consent, privacy and escalation procedures.

05Estimate TAM, SAM and SOM Using a Bottom-Up Model

I would not take the total Indian healthcare market and label a percentage of it as Bolna's market — most healthcare expenditure is unrelated to voice AI software. Instead I would estimate the market for a specific buyer category and use case, starting from public context such as the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare's infrastructure resources and the Ayushman Bharat Digital Mission's facility registry — while recognizing that total registered facilities is not the same as the number of commercial buyers suitable for Bolna.

The assumptions

For an initial planning model, I use these illustrative inputs: potential eligible organizations across India — 20,000; initial serviceable organizations within the chosen segment and operating reach — 5,000; illustrative annual customer spend — ₹6 lakh; three-year target — 150 customers. My principle: TAM should describe the opportunity; SAM should describe who we can serve; SOM should describe what we have a credible plan to win.

06Design the Initial Market-Entry Strategy

I would use a narrow, evidence-led launch rather than a broad healthcare campaign, across three phases.

Phase 1 — Discover the problem (first 30 days)

Interview 15–20 relevant stakeholders — Head of Operations, contact-centre/patient-experience lead, regional operations manager, digital transformation/IT lead, scheduling owner — proposed research targets, not existing Bolna contacts. Questions would cover monthly call volumes, manual-handling share, repetitive workflows, missed-call handling, confirmation/rescheduling processes, required system integrations, disclosure safeguards, trust conditions, metrics that would justify payment, and budget ownership.

Success condition: identify one or two workflows where the problem, buyer, measurable outcome and implementation path are clear.

Phase 2 — Run focused pilots (days 31–60)

Recruit a small number of qualified design partners for a controlled pilot. Initial use case: appointment confirmation and reminders, with scheduling inquiries added only where system access and operational safeguards are feasible. A pilot should define eligible call types, expected volume, languages/scenarios, human escalation rules, approved information and scripts, data-handling/consent requirements, baseline performance, and success metrics with a review date. I would seek paid pilots where possible.

Phase 3 — Convert proof into a repeatable offer (days 61–90+)

Review pilot outcomes, document objections, standardize implementation and develop a healthcare-specific sales narrative. If evidence is positive, build a repeatable package for the chosen ICP rather than customizing every deployment. If evidence is weak, investigate whether the problem is insufficient pain, low willingness to pay, technical complexity or a poor initial use case. The decision to scale should follow evidence, not a predetermined launch calendar.

07Build the Healthcare-Specific Value Proposition

Proposed positioning (a hypothesis to validate, not a claim of guaranteed results): for multi-location diagnostic providers handling significant appointment-related call volumes, Bolna can be evaluated as a way to automate eligible routine voice interactions, improve call handling and reduce manual workload — while escalating exceptions to human staff.

Value by stakeholder: the Head of Operations wants consistent processes and fewer manual tasks; the contact-centre lead wants better routine-call handling and manageable exceptions; the IT/digital transformation lead wants reliable integration, monitoring, security and predictable implementation effort; the finance leader wants evidence that operational benefit justifies software, usage, integration and support costs.

Proposed workflow: the patient receives a reminder or contacts the provider; the agent identifies the permitted purpose of the call; confirms or communicates only approved information; handles the defined routine task or routes to the appropriate team; follows the escalation procedure for clinical questions or uncertainty; and records the outcome for operational review, subject to privacy and retention requirements. The goal is not to replace clinical judgment — it is to automate suitable administrative interactions while keeping appropriate human control.

08Build the Go-to-Market Motion

A new vertical requires more than a lead list — it needs a repeatable way to reach buyers, prove value and expand within successful accounts, across three channels.

Channel 1 — Account-based outbound: a named-account list of the highest-priority diagnostic chains and healthcare groups, with outreach focused on the business problem, not a generic AI pitch — e.g. "Many multi-location diagnostic providers rely on teams to manage appointment confirmations and routine patient calls. I'm exploring whether a voice AI workflow could reduce manual calling effort while maintaining clear human escalation. Would it be worth comparing this with your current process?"

Channel 2 — Strategic partnerships: healthcare technology and implementation firms, patient-engagement and contact-centre solution providers, scheduling/practice-management platform ecosystems, and healthcare operations consultants — prioritized only when they provide credible buyer access and a practical delivery path.

Channel 3 — Use-case-led content: practical material on evaluating appointment-reminder automation, measuring missed-call recovery, designing safe human escalation, calculating the cost of manual confirmations, and evaluating a voice AI pilot before expanding — including where automation would not be appropriate.

Initial sales motion: identify the target account → confirm likely workflow and stakeholder → conduct discovery → estimate the business case from customer-provided baseline data → agree a narrow pilot → measure results against baseline → convert successful pilots into commercial deployment → expand to additional workflows or locations only after validation.

09Develop the Commercial Model and Prove Customer ROI

A healthcare provider will not buy because voice AI sounds innovative — the buyer needs to understand the operational and financial value, built from the customer's own existing workflow data.

Example ROI model (purely illustrative)

Assume 20,000 eligible routine calls per month, 3 minutes of staff time per call, ₹180 fully loaded staff cost per hour, and 50% of eligible calls successfully handled by automation without additional staff intervention.

Current workload = 20,000 calls × 3 min = 60,000 min = 1,000 staff-hours / month
Equivalent monthly staff cost = 1,000 hrs × ₹180 = ₹1,80,000
Capacity released at 50% automation = 500 hrs = ₹90,000 of staff time

This is not automatically a ₹90,000 cash saving — it is a potential capacity benefit. Realized financial savings depend on how the provider uses the released time, staffing arrangements and whether other costs increase.

Net monthly benefit = realized operational savings + measured incremental contribution − software & usage costs − incremental support costs

For each pilot I would capture: current call volume, average handling time, missed-call/abandonment rate, eligible call types, human escalation rate, successful task-completion rate, solution cost, and post-deployment operational outcomes — with baseline and post-deployment periods adjusted for major changes in call volume or operating conditions.

Commercial decision: pursue scale only when the customer sees a defensible benefit, is willing to pay for it, and Bolna can deliver the workflow at acceptable cost and quality.

10Three-Year Growth Plan and Scorecard

Year 1 — Validate the segment: complete discovery interviews, build and validate the named-account universe, run focused pilots, establish implementation/support requirements, test willingness to pay, document outcomes and objections. Decision gate: do target buyers have a sufficiently important problem, and can Bolna solve it reliably with commercially viable economics?

Year 2 — Repeat the sales motion: standardize the pilot and implementation package, build repeatable outbound and partner channels, improve qualification and forecasting, develop customer proof with permission, expand to similar accounts where the use case transfers. Decision gate: can the same approach produce multiple wins without a disproportionate increase in sales and delivery effort?

Year 3 — Scale selectively: expand within proven segments, add workflows supported by customer evidence, develop partner-sourced opportunities, improve retention/adoption/expansion, reassess adjacent-segment economics. Decision gate: does incremental growth continue to generate attractive customer economics and manageable implementation complexity?

The operating dashboard

Market and pipeline: verified target accounts, qualified opportunities, pipeline value, source-channel conversion, sales-cycle duration. Customer acquisition: pilot-to-paid conversion, new customers acquired, CAC, time from first meeting to activation. I would review by cohort and use case — aggregate growth alone could conceal poor unit economics or declining service quality.

The strategic principle:

Enter with one valuable use case, prove the economics, build a repeatable sales motion, and scale only where the evidence supports it.

I would recommend a focused, evidence-led entry into multi-location private diagnostic chains, beginning with appointment confirmations and reminders — without committing to the full market-size estimate or three-year customer target until the account universe, annual spend, customer demand and delivery economics have been validated. The first milestone is not to capture a fixed percentage of the market; it is to prove that one defined customer segment has a repeatable problem, that Bolna can solve it reliably, and that the resulting commercial model works for both the customer and the business.
