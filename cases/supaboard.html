How I Would Acquire 80 New Customers for Supaboard.ai in 6 Months

Case study 7 of 7 in my "Proof of Thinking" series — demonstrating how I reason through growth problems using real frameworks, disclosed assumptions and no fabricated data.

Important Disclaimer

This is an independent hypothetical business case based on publicly available information about Supaboard.ai and general B2B customer-acquisition principles. It is not affiliated with Supaboard and does not use its internal sales, product or customer data.

All targets, funnel volumes, conversion rates and customer-acquisition figures in this case study are hypothetical planning assumptions. They are not Supaboard's actual targets, internal data or results.

01My Understanding of the Opportunity

Supaboard is an AI-powered data analytics platform that helps businesses ask questions about their data, generate dashboards, and automate reports without depending on analysts for every query.

I believe the strongest opportunity is to target companies where teams frequently wait for data analysts or engineers to answer business questions.

My initial focus would be on B2B SaaS and technology companies, followed by e-commerce businesses.

Objective: Acquire 80 new paying B2B customers within 6 months using targeted outbound sales, product-led growth, and strategic partnerships — without significantly increasing the marketing budget.

02Who I Would Target

I would prioritize companies with approximately 50–1,000 employees that use multiple business tools and have an established need for analytics.

Primary decision-makers

  • Head of Data / Analytics
  • BI or Analytics Manager
  • CTO / Head of Data Engineering
  • VP of Revenue Operations
  • COO at smaller companies

I would prioritize companies showing clear reporting bottlenecks rather than contacting businesses based on employee count alone.

03My Customer Acquisition Strategy

Channel 1 — Targeted Outbound Sales

I would build a list of 400 relevant companies using LinkedIn, company websites, job postings, and technology or business signals.

For each company, I would identify four relevant stakeholders, giving me a total of 1,600 targeted stakeholders — a deliberately broader, more multi-threaded reach per account than a single-contact approach, since analytics purchase decisions usually touch more than one function.

I would personalize outreach around specific problems such as slow reporting, growing analytics backlogs, or difficulty combining data from different tools.

I would use LinkedIn, email, and founder-led introductions to book discovery calls and product demos.

Channel 2 — Product-Led Growth

Supaboard offers a free starting option, so I would use it to reduce the barrier to trying the product.

Instead of only asking prospects to book a demo, I would invite qualified prospects to connect a relevant data source, test a real business question, and experience the product's value.

I would follow up with users who activate the product, understand their use case, and help convert successful trials into paid accounts.

Channel 3 — Strategic Partnerships

I would approach boutique data consultancies, analytics agencies, fractional CTOs, and data implementation partners.

These partners already work with businesses facing reporting and analytics challenges. I would explore referral partnerships and joint demos where Supaboard fits their clients' needs.

I would start with a small group of relevant partners and scale only those who generate qualified opportunities.

My decision: Lead with a wider, multi-threaded outbound net (four stakeholders per company rather than one) so a single champion going quiet doesn't stall the account — while product-led trials and partner referrals supply a second and third source of qualified demand that isn't dependent on cold outreach alone.

04My Six-Month Execution Plan

TimelineKey activities
Month 1Learn the product, study existing customers, validate ICP, build the 400-company list, and launch outreach experiments.
Month 2Identify the best-performing segment and messaging; run discovery calls and product demos.
Months 3–4Scale the strongest outbound motion, improve trial activation, and launch partner referrals.
Months 5–6Focus on qualified opportunities, convert active trials, follow up on stalled deals, and build a repeatable acquisition process.

05Funnel and Target

My initial working funnel would be:

MetricSix-month target
Target companies researched400
Relevant stakeholders identified1,600
Discovery meetings320
Qualified opportunities160
Activated trials / structured evaluations120
New paying customers80

These are planning assumptions, not guaranteed conversion rates. I would review actual results in the first 30 days and revise the funnel based on observed conversion, sales-cycle length, and trial activation.

The target assumes approximately 50% of discovery meetings become qualified opportunities, 75% of qualified opportunities reach an activated trial or structured evaluation, and around 67% of those evaluations convert into paying customers. Reaching this volume of discovery meetings off a wider, four-contact-per-company stakeholder base is itself an ambitious assumption that I would need to validate early — if meeting volume falls short, I would rather tighten the ICP than inflate the company list.

400 companies × 4 stakeholders = 1,600 targeted stakeholders

06How I Would Measure Success

I would track five core metrics weekly:

  • Qualified meetings booked.
  • Qualified opportunities created.
  • Trial activation and conversion to paid accounts.
  • Customer acquisition cost by channel.
  • New paying customers and annual recurring revenue.

I would also record why prospects convert or drop out, so the team can improve targeting, messaging, onboarding, and sales conversations.

I would separately track acquisition by channel — outbound, product-led trial, and partner referral — so the plan doesn't quietly become dependent on outbound alone. If trials and partner referrals underperform, that's a signal to adjust the mix, not just push harder on cold outreach.

07My Expected Outcome

My goal would be to acquire 80 new paying customers in six months and establish a repeatable acquisition process that Supaboard can continue scaling.

I would start with a narrow ICP, test channels with limited spending, focus on prospects with clear business pain, and invest more effort only in approaches that demonstrate real conversion.

The principle behind my strategy:

Find the right customers, demonstrate value through their own data, and build a predictable process to convert that value into revenue.
